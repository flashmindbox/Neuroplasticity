# Evidence grading

[← Handbook](../README.md)

Every technique in Rewire carries an **evidence grade**. The grade tells you how much to trust it *before* you
test it on yourself. It is not a promise: even grade-A findings are averages, often from university students in
short studies, and real-world effects are usually smaller than lab effects.

| Grade | Meaning | How we use it |
|---|---|---|
| **A** | Supported by meta-analyses or multiple independent replications | Core of the programme |
| **B** | Good randomised trials, fewer replications, or strong theory with consistent support | Use, and confirm with a personal experiment |
| **C** | Mixed or conflicting evidence | Optional; keep only if *your* data says it helps |
| **Analogy** | Borrowed from another field (e.g., sports science), not tested for exam preparation | Use cautiously, labelled as such |
| **Excluded** | Failed replication or no credible evidence | Not used |

## Grade A

- **Implementation intentions** ("If situation X, then I will do Y") — medium-to-large effect on goal attainment across 94 studies (Gollwitzer & Sheeran, 2006).
- **Habit formation through context-stable repetition** — automaticity grows with repetition in a stable context; median 66 days (Lally et al., 2010; Wood & Rünger, 2016).
- **Stress inoculation training** — practising under graded, realistic stress reduces performance anxiety and improves performance under stress (Saunders et al., 1996).
- **Sleep** for memory consolidation and emotional regulation; chronic restriction to 6 h impairs attention as much as a night without sleep, while people underestimate their own impairment (Van Dongen et al., 2003).
- **Aerobic exercise** for executive function and mood (Ludyga et al., 2020; Chang et al., 2012).
- **Calibration / forecasting training** — short training improved real forecasting accuracy (Mellers et al., 2014).
- **Personality change through sustained intervention** — moderate change over ~24 weeks (Roberts et al., 2017); requires actually doing the behaviours (Hudson et al., 2019).
- **Self-efficacy built through mastery experiences** — the strongest source of confidence is succeeding at progressively harder tasks (Bandura, 1977).

## Grade B

- **Mindfulness / attention training** — two weeks improved working memory and GRE reading scores and reduced mind-wandering (Mrazek et al., 2013); protected working memory under stress in soldiers (Jha et al., 2010).
- **Mental contrasting with implementation intentions (WOOP)** (Oettingen, 2012; Duckworth et al., 2013).
- **Process simulation** — imagining the steps beats imagining the outcome (Pham & Taylor, 1999).
- **Affect labelling** — naming an emotion reduces amygdala activity (Lieberman et al., 2007).
- **Arousal reappraisal** — telling yourself that stress arousal helps performance improved GRE maths scores (Jamieson et al., 2010; Brooks, 2014).
- **Self-compassion after failure** increases motivation to improve (Breines & Chen, 2012).
- **Slow breathing / cyclic sighing** reduce physiological arousal and improve mood (Zaccaro et al., 2018; Balban et al., 2023).
- **Learning by teaching** (Nestojko et al., 2014).
- **Explicit critical-thinking instruction plus practice** (Abrami et al., 2015).
- **Distanced self-talk** ("You can handle this, [your name]") for regulating emotion under stress (Kross et al., 2014).

## Grade C — optional, test personally

- **Expressive writing about exam worries before a test** — a *Science* paper found benefits (Ramirez & Beilock, 2011); a 2021 classroom study found no effect on anxiety or scores (Myers et al., 2021).
- **Gratitude journaling** — positive but modest and inconsistent effects (Emmons & McCullough, 2003, and later mixed findings).

## Analogy (sports science)

- **Tapering** before competition improves athletic performance (Bosquet et al., 2007). We borrow the idea of reducing load in the final ~10 days before Prelims and Mains. It has not been tested for exams.
- **Periodisation / deload weeks** — planned lighter weeks to absorb training.

## Downgraded

- **Growth-mindset messaging.** A 2023 meta-analysis concluded that apparent effects on achievement are largely attributable to study-design flaws and bias (Macnamara & Burgoyne, 2023); an earlier one found weak effects (Sisk et al., 2018). Rewire keeps the useful core — *abilities improve with the right practice* — but builds it through real mastery experiences, not slogans.
- **Grit as a separate trait.** Largely overlaps with conscientiousness and predicts performance modestly (Credé et al., 2017). We train *consistency behaviours* instead.

## Excluded

- **Brain-training games** — poor transfer to real abilities (Simons et al., 2016; Sala et al., 2019).
- **Power posing** — hormonal and behavioural effects did not replicate (Ranehill et al., 2015).
- **"Willpower is a limited tank" (ego depletion)** — a large multi-lab replication found an effect close to zero (Hagger et al., 2016). Rewire relies on habits and environment design, not willpower budgeting.
- **"21 days to form a habit"** — contradicted by data (Lally et al., 2010).
- **Learning styles, "left-brain/right-brain" types** — no credible evidence; not used.
