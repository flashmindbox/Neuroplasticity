# How the brain changes

[← Handbook](../README.md)

## The short version

1. **The adult brain stays changeable.** Learning physically changes the brain at every adult age.
2. **It changes in response to what you repeatedly do, with attention, over weeks and months.**
3. **Change is specific.** You get better at the exact thing you practise — not at "intelligence" in general.
4. **Change needs recovery.** Much of the consolidation happens during sleep and rest, not during effort.
5. **Emotion and arousal modulate change.** What feels important (and what you are anxious about) is learned more strongly — for better or worse.

## What the evidence actually shows

**Adult plasticity is real.** London taxi drivers who memorised the city's streets showed measurable
differences in the hippocampus, a memory structure, and those differences grew with years of driving
(Maguire et al., 2000). Adults who learned to juggle showed grey-matter changes in motion-processing areas
within three months, which partly reversed when they stopped practising (Draganski et al., 2004) — and the same
kind of change was found in people over 60 (Boyke et al., 2008). Two lessons: **the brain adapts to demands,
and it un-adapts when the demand stops.** Practice has to continue.

**Change is specific.** The best evidence on "brain training" games shows people get better at the games, with
little or no improvement in untrained real-world abilities (Simons et al., 2016; Sala et al., 2019). This is the
single most important design principle of Rewire: **we train the exact behaviours the exam and the job require**,
in conditions as close to the real ones as possible.

**Behaviour changes personality — slowly.** Personality traits are not fixed. A review of 207 intervention
studies found meaningful trait change over an average of about 24 weeks, most of all in emotional stability
(Roberts et al., 2017) — though most of those studies were of therapy, not self-directed programmes. People who
*want* to change a trait can do so, but only when they actually carry out the trait-consistent behaviours
(Hudson & Fraley, 2015). Crucially, people who signed up for behavioural challenges and **failed** them changed
in the *opposite* direction (Hudson et al., 2019). Overreaching is not neutral.

**Habits take longer than you have been told.** In the best real-world study, a new daily behaviour took a
median of 66 days to become automatic — with a range of 18 to 254 days (Lally et al., 2010). "21 days" is a myth.
Missing a single day did not derail habit formation; patterns of missing did.

**Sleep and exercise are part of the mechanism.** Sleep consolidates what you practised during the day and
regulates emotional reactivity; one night of sleep deprivation made the brain's alarm centre markedly more
reactive (Walker & Stickgold, 2006; Yoo et al., 2007). Regular aerobic exercise improves executive function
(Ludyga et al., 2020) and, in older adults, increased hippocampal volume (Erickson et al., 2011).

## The five laws Rewire is built on

| Law | What it means for you | Where it shows up |
|---|---|---|
| **Specificity** | Practise the real task, in real conditions | Drills use exam-format material; skills are tested in mock conditions |
| **Repetition with attention** | Distracted repetition barely changes the brain | Focus-block protocol; attention training |
| **Feedback & error** | The brain updates most on its mistakes | Error logs, calibration, rubrics, external ratings |
| **Graded difficulty** | Hard enough to stretch, easy enough to succeed ~80% of the time | Levels 1–4 in every module; max 2–3 targets |
| **Consolidation** | Gains are locked in during sleep and rest | Sleep protocol, deload weeks, tapers before exams |

## What this does *not* mean

- It does not mean you can become anything in 21 days.
- It does not mean visualising success "programs" your brain. Imagining the *outcome* alone can reduce effort;
  imagining the *process* helps ([Vision & identity](../modules/L2-vision-identity.md)).
- It does not mean your 30+ brain is too old. See [Brain clarity](../modules/L0-brain-clarity.md).
- It does not mean every technique works for every person. Research gives averages. You are one person —
  so you test techniques on yourself ([Personal experiments](../05-personal-experiments.md)).
