# Programme rules

[← Handbook](../README.md)

Ten rules. Each one exists because ignoring it is a known way for programmes like this to fail.

### 1. Behaviour first, feelings follow
You will not feel disciplined, calm or confident before you act that way. Traits change when you repeatedly
*do* the trait-consistent behaviour (Hudson & Fraley, 2015). Every module is a list of behaviours with doses.

### 2. Maximum 2–3 active targets
People who accepted behavioural challenges and failed to complete them changed in the *opposite* direction
to the one they wanted (Hudson et al., 2019). Overloading yourself is not ambitious — it is counter-productive.
Pick 2–3 targets in your Personal Charter. Add a new one only when an old one is automatic.

### 3. Size challenges for ~80% success
A challenge you complete most days builds the habit and the self-belief. A challenge you fail most days teaches
you that you are someone who fails. If you are under ~70% for two weeks, make it smaller.

### 4. Never miss twice
Missing one day barely affects habit formation (Lally et al., 2010). The danger is the "what-the-hell" reaction —
one lapse becoming a collapse (Marlatt & Gordon, 1985). On bad days do the **minimum viable day** (≈5 minutes).
Never miss two days in a row.

### 5. Measure objectively; distrust your own ratings
Self-ratings track real ability only modestly (Zell & Krizan, 2014). Use the objective markers in each module,
the AI evaluator and, when possible, people. Track the gap between your rating and external ratings.

### 6. Train for transfer
Practise on exam-format material and test every skill in mock conditions. Skills practised in the abstract
rarely transfer (Sala et al., 2019).

### 7. Test techniques on yourself
Research gives averages. Run [personal experiments](../05-personal-experiments.md): baseline → technique → compare.
Keep what works for you; drop what doesn't — even if it is grade A.

### 8. Pre-commit decisions
Decide *now* what you will do when the data says something is wrong, so that mood doesn't decide later.
The app enforces these rules (see [Failure modes](../06-failure-modes.md)):
- No improvement on a target's marker in 4 weeks → change the technique.
- Stress ≥ 7/10 for 7 days → deload week.
- Depression or anxiety screen in the warning range → talk to a professional.
- Unexplained Brain Check decline for 2+ weeks → see a doctor.

### 9. Identity rests on values, not on the result
Self-worth that depends on one outcome is fragile and costly (Crocker & Park, 2004). You are building a person who
serves, thinks clearly and keeps promises — that person exists whatever the rank. Write your Plan B; it protects
Plan A by lowering the threat.

### 10. Recovery is part of training
Sleep, exercise, a weekly half-day off, a lighter week roughly every six weeks and a taper before each exam are
scheduled, not earned.
