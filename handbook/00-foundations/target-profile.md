# Target profile — the 13 dimensions

[← Handbook](../README.md)

## Where the profile comes from

The profile is anchored in UPSC's own words, not in motivational folklore.

**The Personality Test.** UPSC states that the interview assesses "the personal suitability of the candidate for
a career in public service", and lists qualities including **mental alertness, critical powers of assimilation,
clear and logical exposition, balance of judgement, variety and depth of interest, ability for social cohesion
and leadership, intellectual and moral integrity.**

**GS Paper IV** asks about aptitude and foundational values for the civil service: **integrity, impartiality and
non-partisanship, objectivity, dedication to public service, empathy, tolerance and compassion towards the
weaker sections**, and **emotional intelligence**.

**The written exam** silently tests more: sustained attention for 3-hour papers, judgement under uncertainty with
negative marking, structured expression at speed, and composure across five days of Mains.

These map onto 13 trainable dimensions in four clusters.

## How to rate yourself

Each dimension has four behaviourally anchored levels. Rate the level whose description is **consistently true
in the last 4 weeks** — not on your best day. The app also records an *external* rating (AI evaluator, or a
person) where one is possible; the **gap between your rating and the external rating** is itself a measure of
self-awareness. People's self-ratings correlate only modestly with their actual performance (Zell & Krizan, 2014),
so treat the objective markers as the tie-breaker.

Monthly ratings use a 1–10 scale in the app: levels 1–4 map to roughly 1–2, 3–5, 6–8 and 9–10.

## Cluster A — Self-regulation

### 1. Deep focus
*Why UPSC cares:* every paper is 2–3 hours of sustained, accurate attention; mind-wandering costs marks silently.
- **L1** Focus breaks within 10–15 minutes; phone checks are frequent and automatic.
- **L2** 25-minute blocks with ≤3 noticed mind-wanders; phone out of reach during blocks.
- **L3** 50–90-minute blocks routinely; notice and return quickly; 4+ deep-work hours on most days.
- **L4** Full 3-hour exam-simulation blocks with stable accuracy in the last 30 minutes.
- *Markers:* focus minutes/day, mind-wander tally per block, sustained-attention test, quality drop in final 30 min of simulations.

### 2. Discipline & habits
*Why:* ten months of preparation are won by the median day, not the best day.
- **L1** Plans often abandoned; days depend on mood.
- **L2** Keystone habits (wake time, focus blocks, exercise) done on most days; misses are followed by return.
- **L3** Habits automatic; minimum viable day used instead of zero days; never two misses in a row.
- **L4** System runs through illness, travel and bad news with planned adjustments, not collapse.
- *Markers:* habit completion %, never-miss-twice streak, wake-time variance.

### 3. Stress & emotional regulation
*Why:* anxiety consumes working memory exactly when you need it (Beilock & Carr, 2005); the Personality Test probes composure.
- **L1** Stress spirals into avoidance, rumination or poor sleep.
- **L2** You can name what you feel and use one regulation tool (breathing, reappraisal) when prompted.
- **L3** You regulate in the moment without prompting; bad mock scores are processed within a day.
- **L4** High-stakes situations feel like energy; you perform at or near your practice level under pressure.
- *Markers:* stress 1–10 trend, PSS-style monthly check, mock-day vs practice-day performance gap.

## Cluster B — Mindset

### 4. Vision & purpose
*Why:* a concrete, personally owned "why" sustains effort through the long middle months.
- **L1** "I want to clear UPSC" — no concrete picture of the work or why it matters to you.
- **L2** A written purpose statement; you can describe the kind of officer you want to be.
- **L3** A vivid, specific picture of the daily work (district, problems, people) linked to today's tasks.
- **L4** Purpose guides choices automatically; you can articulate it convincingly in 60 seconds to a board.
- *Markers:* purpose statement written and revised; 60-second "why" talk rated.

### 5. Motivation & drive
*Why:* motivation will dip; the system must carry you through dips.
- **L1** Effort depends on how you feel; dips last weeks.
- **L2** You start with the 2-minute rule on low days; dips last days.
- **L3** You diagnose dips (purpose / progress / connection / energy) and fix the specific cause.
- **L4** Effort itself feels rewarding; dips are anticipated and short.
- *Markers:* focus minutes on low-mood days, dip length, weekly effort trend.

### 6. Confidence (self-efficacy)
*Why:* belief in your ability to do *specific* tasks predicts persistence and performance (Bandura, 1977).
- **L1** Confidence swings with each score; frequent "I can't".
- **L2** Confidence based on a log of evidence (completed challenges).
- **L3** You take on stretch tasks voluntarily; failures don't change your self-assessment much.
- **L4** Calm, evidence-based confidence visible in posture, voice and decisions.
- *Markers:* mastery-ladder level, evidence-log entries, stretch challenges attempted.

### 7. Resilience
*Why:* most toppers have absorbed failures; setbacks are guaranteed.
- **L1** A setback derails you for a week or more.
- **L2** You use the failure protocol and return within 2–3 days.
- **L3** You extract a specific lesson from each setback within 24 hours.
- **L4** Setbacks become fuel; your identity does not depend on any single outcome.
- *Markers:* recovery time after bad days, failure-protocol entries, self-compassion check.

## Cluster C — Thinking

### 8. Critical & multi-angle thinking
*Why:* Mains answers and the Essay reward multidimensional, balanced analysis.
- **L1** One or two angles; opinions stated without reasons.
- **L2** 4–5 angles in 90 seconds using the 7-lens drill; you can state the other side.
- **L3** 7+ relevant angles, causal chains, and a steelman of the opposing view without prompting.
- **L4** You find the non-obvious angle and the trade-off at the heart of the issue, quickly.
- *Markers:* angles per 90 s (blind-rated), steelman quality score, AI rubric score.

### 9. Judgement under uncertainty
*Why:* Prelims negative marking punishes both overconfidence and timidity; the board tests balance of judgement.
- **L1** Attempts based on gut feel; no idea how accurate your confidence is.
- **L2** You tag confidence on every mock answer and know your accuracy per bucket.
- **L3** Your confidence matches your accuracy within ~10 points; you follow a personal attempt rule.
- **L4** Well-calibrated under time pressure; decisions are explicit about probabilities and trade-offs.
- *Markers:* calibration gap per bucket, Brier score, EV per bucket.

### 10. Clear, structured expression
*Why:* graders read hundreds of copies; structure is how your thinking becomes visible.
- **L1** Ideas come out in the order you think of them.
- **L2** You use a structure (intro–body–way forward) when you remember to.
- **L3** Structure is automatic in writing and speech; one idea per paragraph; clear signposting.
- **L4** Crisp, layered answers at exam speed; you adapt structure to the directive word.
- *Markers:* structure score on drills, words/min at quality, AI rubric score.

## Cluster D — Personality

### 11. Communication & presence
*Why:* 275 marks of the final 2025 are a conversation.
- **L1** Hesitant, filler-heavy speech; avoids speaking.
- **L2** 2-minute talks with a clear point; filler words noticed and reducing.
- **L3** Calm pace, eye contact, concise answers; you listen before answering.
- **L4** Warm, composed, genuinely conversational under questioning; you say "I don't know" gracefully.
- *Markers:* fillers/min, words/min, recorded-talk rubric (self vs external), mock-interview score.

### 12. Leadership & social cohesion
*Why:* explicitly assessed by the board; real stories make Essay and GS4 answers authentic.
- **L1** Studies in isolation; no current leadership role.
- **L2** Has an accountability partner; helps others occasionally.
- **L3** Runs a study circle or a regular service activity; resolves a conflict constructively.
- **L4** Others seek you out; you have several true, specific leadership stories with reflections.
- *Markers:* service actions/week, leadership-story log, partner check-ins kept.

### 13. Integrity, empathy & civil-servant identity
*Why:* GS4 and the board test values; identity sustains behaviour when no one is watching.
- **L1** Values are abstract; little exposure to lives very different from yours.
- **L2** You reflect weekly on an ethical dilemma and on someone else's perspective.
- **L3** Regular exposure to field realities; you can reason through dilemmas with stakeholders and principles.
- **L4** Values show in daily choices (honest logs, keeping promises); your identity rests on character, not rank.
- *Markers:* dilemma reflections, honest-logging checks, empathy-exposure entries.
