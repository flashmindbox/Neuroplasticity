# Personal experiments

[← Handbook](README.md)

Research findings are averages — mostly from university students in short studies. You are one person in a
ten-month, high-stakes situation. So every technique that matters gets **tested on you**. This is a recognised
method (single-case or "n-of-1" design), not a hack.

## The protocol

1. **Question:** "Does *[technique]* improve *[one marker]* for me?"
2. **Choose the marker before you start** (clarity rating, focus minutes, mind-wanders per block, stress, Brain Check score…).
   Choosing afterwards lets you fool yourself.
3. **Baseline (1–2 weeks):** live normally; log the marker daily.
4. **Intervention (2–3 weeks):** add *only* the technique. Change nothing else if you can.
5. **Compare:** the app shows baseline vs intervention averages and the difference.
6. **Decide:** keep, drop, or re-test. Write one sentence on why.

## Good first experiments

| Experiment | Marker |
|---|---|
| **Fog audit** (Phase 0): log clarity alongside sleep, caffeine, screens, exercise | Clarity 1–10 |
| Caffeine cut-off 8–10 h before bed | Sleep hours; next-day clarity |
| Morning exercise vs evening exercise | Focus minutes; clarity |
| Phone in another room vs on the desk | Mind-wanders per block |
| 10-min attention training before the first study block | Mind-wanders in the first block |
| Expressive writing before mocks (grade C) | Mock score vs your recent average; pre-mock stress |
| Screens off 60 min before bed | Sleep hours; morning clarity |

## Honest limits

- **Expectation effects:** believing a technique works can improve your ratings. Prefer objective markers
  (focus minutes, Brain Check, mock accuracy) over feelings where possible.
- **Noise:** day-to-day variation is large. Prefer differences you see in both weeks of the intervention.
- **Confounds:** exams, illness or bad news during an experiment muddy the result. Note them; re-run if needed.
- **One at a time.** Two simultaneous experiments can't be separated.
