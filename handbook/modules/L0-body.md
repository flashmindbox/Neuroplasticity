# L0 · Body & recovery

[← Handbook](../README.md)

**Dimensions served:** all — especially 1 Deep focus, 3 Stress & emotional regulation, 5 Motivation.
**Evidence grade:** A (sleep, aerobic exercise) · B (slow breathing) · Analogy (deload & taper).

## Why it matters for UPSC
Your brain is the hardware every other module runs on. Two weeks of 6-hour nights impair attention about as
much as a full night without sleep — and people in that state report feeling only slightly sleepy
(Van Dongen et al., 2003). You cannot out-discipline a sleep debt.

## How it works
- **Sleep** consolidates what you practised during the day and resets emotional reactivity; without it, the
  brain's threat system overreacts (Walker & Stickgold, 2006; Yoo et al., 2007).
- **Aerobic exercise** improves executive functions — planning, inhibition, working memory — with lasting
  training and after single sessions (Ludyga et al., 2020; Chang et al., 2012).
- **Circadian timing**: alertness follows your body clock. A fixed wake time and morning light anchor it.
  Exams start at 9:30 and 14:30 — train your clock to be sharp then.
- **Caffeine** has a long half-life; even 6 hours before bed it measurably reduced sleep (Drake et al., 2013).
- **Mild dehydration** worsened attention and mood in a controlled study (Ganio et al., 2011).

## Exercises & dose
| Practice | Dose | Grade |
|---|---|---|
| Fixed wake time (± 30 min, including Sundays) | Daily | A |
| Sleep opportunity of 7.5–8.5 h in bed | Daily | A |
| Outdoor daylight soon after waking | 5–15 min daily | B |
| Aerobic exercise (brisk walk, run, cycle, sport) | 30–45 min, ≥ 5 days/week | A |
| Movement break between study blocks | 2–5 min each | B |
| Caffeine cut-off | ≥ 8 h before bed (test it — [experiment](../05-personal-experiments.md)) | A |
| Water bottle on desk | Daily | B |
| Screens off before bed | 45–60 min | B |
| Weekly half-day fully off | Weekly | B |
| Deload week (programme dose halved) | ~ every 6th week | Analogy |
| Taper before Prelims and Mains | Final ~10 days | Analogy |

## Progression
- **L1** Wake time varies by > 1 hour; exercise occasional.
- **L2** Fixed wake time 5+ days/week; exercise 3+ days/week.
- **L3** 7+ hours sleep on most nights; exercise 5+ days/week; caffeine cut-off held.
- **L4** Exam-time alertness rehearsed; recovery planned around mocks and travel.

## Objective markers
Sleep hours (logged), wake-time variance, exercise minutes/week, clarity ratings, Brain Check trend.

## Common traps
- Cutting sleep to "gain" study hours — you lose more in quality than you gain in time.
- Treating exercise as optional on busy days — busy days are when you need it most.
- Weekend lie-ins that shift your clock (social jet lag).
