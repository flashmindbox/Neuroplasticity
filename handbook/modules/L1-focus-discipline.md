# L1 · Focus & discipline

[← Handbook](../README.md)

**Dimensions served:** 1 Deep focus, 2 Discipline & habits.
**Evidence grade:** A (implementation intentions, habit formation) · B (attention training, environment design).

## Why it matters for UPSC
A Mains paper is 3 hours; Mains is two papers a day for five days. Prelims GS is 2 hours of 100 decisions.
People's minds wander during roughly half of their waking activities (Killingsworth & Gilbert, 2010) — in an
exam, each wander is a misread statement or a lost minute. And ten months of preparation are decided by your
*median* day, which is a habit problem, not a motivation problem.

## How it works
- **Attention is trainable as a skill.** Two weeks of mindfulness-based attention training reduced
  mind-wandering and improved working memory and GRE reading scores (Mrazek et al., 2013), and protected working
  memory under high stress (Jha et al., 2010). The core skill is *noticing* that you've drifted and *returning*.
- **Switching has a cost.** After switching tasks, attention stays partly on the previous one ("attention
  residue"; Leroy, 2009). Phone checks create constant residue.
- **Habits are cue → routine in a stable context.** Repetition in the same context makes behaviour automatic,
  reducing the need for willpower (Wood & Rünger, 2016; Lally et al., 2010).
- **If-then plans** link a situation to a response so the decision is made in advance (Gollwitzer & Sheeran, 2006).
- **Environment beats willpower.** Removing cues and adding friction to distractions is more reliable than
  resisting them; the "willpower tank" idea failed to replicate (Hagger et al., 2016).

## Exercises & dose

### 1. Attention training (grade B) — 10 → 20 min daily, morning
Sit upright. Attend to the sensation of breathing. When you notice your mind has wandered, silently note
"wandering" and return. **Each return is one repetition** — the wandering is not failure, the return is the training.
Weeks 1–2: 10 min. Weeks 3–6: 15 min. Then 20 min.

### 2. Focus-block protocol (grade B) — every study block
1. Write the block's **intention** (one line: what "done" looks like).
2. **Phone in another room** (or in a locked box). Not on the desk, not face-down.
3. Tally each **noticed mind-wander** (a mark on paper or the app's tally button). Noticing is the skill.
4. At the end: **one-line review** (done? quality 1–5? what pulled you away?).
Progression: 25 → 50 → 90 → 180 min (the last in exam simulations only).

### 3. Implementation intentions (grade A) — one per day, one per obstacle
Format: **"If [situation], then I will [specific action]."**
- "If I feel the urge to check my phone during a block, then I will write the urge on paper and continue."
- "If I wake up and want to snooze, then I will put my feet on the floor and open the curtains."
- "If I finish a block early, then I will start the next item on the list, not a break."

### 4. Keystone habits (grade A) — install in Phase 0
Fixed wake time · phone out of the study room · first study block starts at the same time · nightly 5-minute
journal. Attach each to an existing cue ("after I brush my teeth, I…").

### 5. Environment design (grade B)
Study in the same place; desk holds only today's material; app blockers on phone and laptop; notifications off;
social apps logged out (friction); a visible "focus block in progress" sign for family.

### 6. Minimum viable day & never miss twice (grade A, see Programme rules)

## Progression
- **L1** 25-min blocks with phone in another room; attention training 10 min; 1 if-then plan/day.
- **L2** 50-min blocks; ≤ 3 noticed wanders per block; habit completion ≥ 70%.
- **L3** 90-min blocks; 4+ deep hours/day; habits ≥ 85%; never two misses in a row.
- **L4** 3-hour simulations with stable accuracy in the final 30 minutes.

## Objective markers
Focus minutes/day · mind-wanders per block (should *rise* at first as you notice more, then fall) · habit
completion % · never-miss-twice streak · sustained-attention test (monthly).

## Common traps
- Counting hours at the desk instead of focused minutes.
- Treating a wander as failure (it's the repetition).
- Starting with 90-minute blocks — start where you can succeed ~80% of the time.
- "Just checking one thing" on the phone between blocks.
