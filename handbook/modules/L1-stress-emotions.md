# L1 · Stress & emotions

[← Handbook](../README.md)

**Dimensions served:** 3 Stress & emotional regulation, 7 Resilience, 11 Communication & presence.
**Evidence grade:** A (stress inoculation, sleep) · B (affect labelling, reappraisal, slow breathing, distanced self-talk) · C (expressive writing).

## Why it matters for UPSC
Worry occupies working memory — the same resource you need to hold statements, eliminate options and structure
answers. Under pressure, people with the most working memory can "choke" the most (Beilock & Carr, 2005). The
Personality Test explicitly watches composure. Stress is not the enemy; *unmanaged* stress is.

## How it works
- **Naming an emotion** ("I feel anxious", "I feel ashamed") reduces amygdala activity — affect labelling
  (Lieberman et al., 2007).
- **Reappraising arousal** — telling yourself that a racing heart is your body preparing to perform — improved
  GRE maths scores (Jamieson et al., 2010); saying "I am excited" beat "I am calm" before performances (Brooks, 2014).
- **Slow breathing** with long exhales reduces physiological arousal; daily cyclic sighing improved mood more
  than mindfulness in one trial (Zaccaro et al., 2018; Balban et al., 2023).
- **Distanced self-talk** — addressing yourself by name or as "you" — improves emotion regulation under social
  stress (Kross et al., 2014).
- **Stress inoculation** — graded exposure to realistic stressors while practising coping — reduces anxiety and
  improves performance under stress (Saunders et al., 1996).
- **Sleep** resets emotional reactivity (Yoo et al., 2007).

## Exercises & dose

### 1. Name it to tame it (B) — whenever you notice a strong feeling; nightly in the journal
Pick the most precise word (the app's emotion list helps): not "bad" but "embarrassed", "dread", "envious", "flat".

### 2. Cyclic sighing (B) — 1–5 min; between blocks, before mocks, before sleep
Two inhales through the nose (the second a short top-up), then a long, slow exhale through the mouth. Repeat.
The app's breath pacer guides you.

### 3. Arousal reappraisal (B) — before every mock and the real exam
Script: "My heart is beating faster because my body is getting ready. This energy helps me focus."
Practise it before mocks so it is automatic on exam day.

### 4. Distanced self-talk (B) — when anxious or after a setback
"[Your name], you have prepared for this. What's the next small step?"

### 5. Worry window (B) — 15 min, same time daily, not near bedtime
Write worries down during the day; process them only in the window: solvable → if-then plan; unsolvable → let go.

### 6. Stress inoculation (A) — graded across the year
Level 1: timed drills. Level 2: full mocks at exam time. Level 3: mocks with a deliberate disruption (noise,
a harder paper, a late start). Level 4: the 5-day Mains rehearsal.

### 7. Expressive writing before mocks (C) — optional experiment
10 minutes writing freely about your worries right before a mock. Evidence is mixed (Ramirez & Beilock, 2011;
Myers et al., 2021) — run it as a [personal experiment](../05-personal-experiments.md).

## Progression
- **L1** You name feelings in the nightly journal; one breathing tool known.
- **L2** You use breathing and reappraisal when prompted; stress trend stable.
- **L3** You regulate in the moment without prompting; mock-day performance close to practice.
- **L4** Pressure feels like energy; composure under questioning.

## Objective markers
Stress 1–10 trend · PHQ-9/GAD-7 trend · mock score vs recent practice average · sleep hours.

## Common traps
- Trying to *suppress* anxiety ("don't panic") — it backfires. Name it and reframe it.
- Only practising calm on calm days. The skill is built under graded pressure.
- Using the worry window at night.
