# L2 · Confidence & resilience

[← Handbook](../README.md)

**Dimensions served:** 6 Confidence, 7 Resilience, 3 Stress & emotional regulation.
**Evidence grade:** A (self-efficacy via mastery, volitional change requires follow-through) · B (self-compassion) · Downgraded (growth-mindset slogans) · Excluded (power posing).

## Why it matters for UPSC
Confidence decides whether you attempt the tough question, stretch your answer, and sit calmly in front of a
board. Resilience decides what happens after a bad mock, a failed Prelims or a harsh evaluation. Both can be
built — but not by affirmations.

## How it works
- **Self-efficacy** — belief in your ability to do a *specific* task — comes mostly from **mastery experiences**:
  succeeding at progressively harder versions of the task (Bandura, 1977). Next: seeing similar people succeed;
  encouragement; and interpreting your body's arousal well.
- **Failure without follow-through erodes self-concept.** People who took on change challenges and failed them
  shifted the wrong way (Hudson et al., 2019). Hence the ~80% success rule.
- **Self-compassion after failure** increased the motivation to improve, more than self-esteem boosting
  (Breines & Chen, 2012). Being harsh with yourself does not make you work harder.
- **What we don't use:** power poses (did not replicate — Ranehill et al., 2015) and growth-mindset slogans
  (effects largely attributable to study flaws — Macnamara & Burgoyne, 2023).

## Exercises & dose

### 1. Mastery ladder (A) — for each active target
Write 4–6 rungs from "easy for me now" to "topper-grade". Climb one rung only after ~80% success for a week.
Example for speaking: 1-min talk alone → 2-min recorded → 2-min to a friend → 5-min with questions → mock board.

### 2. Evidence log (A/B) — ongoing
Log completed challenges. Before a mock or interview, read the last ten entries. Confidence built on evidence
survives bad days.

### 3. Weekly stretch challenge (A) — one per week
Slightly outside your comfort zone and winnable: ask a question in a seminar, lead a study-circle session, call
someone you've been avoiding.

### 4. Failure protocol (B) — within 24 hours of any setback
1. **Name it:** what happened, and what you feel (affect labelling).
2. **Self-compassion:** "This is hard. Everyone preparing for this fails at things. What would I say to a friend?"
3. **Extract:** one specific, controllable lesson.
4. **Plan:** one if-then for next time.
5. **Return:** the next scheduled block happens as planned.

### 5. Comparison hygiene (B)
Mute score-sharing groups around mocks. Compare yourself with your own 4-week trend.

### 6. Plan B (B) — see [Vision & identity](L2-vision-identity.md)
Lowering the threat of failure frees the working memory that fear would occupy.

## Progression
- **L1** Evidence log started; failure protocol known.
- **L2** One stretch challenge/week; recovery within 2–3 days.
- **L3** Mastery ladders climbing; lessons extracted within 24 hours.
- **L4** Calm, evidence-based confidence; setbacks don't change your self-assessment.

## Objective markers
Mastery-ladder rung per target · stretch challenges/month · recovery time after setbacks (days) · evidence-log entries.

## Common traps
- Affirmations without evidence.
- Comparing your behind-the-scenes with others' highlight reels.
- Treating one mock as a verdict.
