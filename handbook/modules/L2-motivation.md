# L2 · Motivation engine

[← Handbook](../README.md)

**Dimensions served:** 5 Motivation & drive, 2 Discipline, 4 Vision & purpose.
**Evidence grade:** A (self-determination theory, implementation intentions) · B (progress principle, goal gradient, fresh start, temptation bundling, learned industriousness).

## Why it matters for UPSC
Motivation **will** dip. Over ten months, the dips are predictable:
- **Jan–Mar — the messy middle.** The novelty is gone and the exam still feels far away.
- **After Prelims** — the wait for results, and doubt.
- **July** — Mains fatigue.

The goal is not to *feel* motivated every day. It is a **system that keeps you working when you don't**.

## How it works
- **Autonomy, competence, relatedness.** Motivation that lasts comes from choosing your path, feeling yourself
  improve, and feeling connected to people (Ryan & Deci, 2000).
- **The brain's reward system tracks expected progress**, not just final outcomes (reward prediction — Schultz
  et al., 1997). Visible daily progress feeds it; distant rank alone does not. Small wins in meaningful work are
  the strongest day-to-day motivator (Amabile & Kramer, 2011).
- **Goal gradient:** effort rises as a finish line approaches (Kivetz et al., 2006) → short sprints.
- **Fresh starts:** new beginnings (a Monday, a new month, a new phase) increase goal-directed behaviour
  (Dai et al., 2014).
- **Temptation bundling:** pairing a pleasure *only* with a hard task increased exercise (Milkman et al., 2014).
- **Learned industriousness:** when effort itself is rewarded, effort becomes less aversive (Eisenberger, 1992).
- **Action creates motivation:** starting is the hardest part; a tiny start often carries on.

## Exercises & dose

### 1. Visible progress (B) — daily
Log focus minutes and completed items. The app's progress chart is a motivation tool, not decoration.
Write tonight's **one win**, however small.

### 2. Reward effort, not just scores (B) — weekly
Celebrate focus minutes and kept habits, separately from mock scores. Scores fluctuate; effort is yours.

### 3. Two-week sprints (B) — continuous
A concrete finish line each sprint; a WOOP at the start ([Vision & identity](L2-vision-identity.md)); a fresh-start
review at the end.

### 4. Two-minute start (B) — whenever you are stuck
"I will only do 2 minutes." Open the book, write the first line. Most of the time you'll continue.

### 5. Temptation bundling (B) — pick one
A favourite tea, playlist or podcast used **only** during a hard task (e.g., answer writing practice, the walk).

### 6. Protect your reward baseline (B) — daily
Short-form video and endless feeds make study feel dull by comparison. Keep them out of study hours entirely;
schedule leisure deliberately instead.

### 7. Dip diagnosis (B) — when motivation drops for 3+ days
Ask which source is lowest and apply the matching fix:

| Low source | Signs | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| **Purpose** | "Why am I doing this?" | Re-read and revise your purpose statement; 10 min of field reality (a news story, a district report) |
| **Progress (competence)** | "I'm not getting better" | Look at your 4-week trends; shrink the challenge; log small wins |
| **Connection** | Isolated, lonely | Call your accountability partner; study circle; one honest conversation |
| **Energy** | Tired, foggy | Sleep, exercise, food, a rest day — this is physiology, not character |

### 8. Fresh-start resets (B)
After a bad patch, pick the next natural start (Monday / 1st of the month / new phase) and restart cleanly.
No catching up on missed days.

## Progression
- **L1** You use the 2-minute start on low days.
- **L2** Daily wins logged; sprints with finish lines.
- **L3** Dips diagnosed and fixed within days.
- **L4** Effort feels rewarding in itself; dips are short and expected.

## Objective markers
Focus minutes on low-mood days · dip length (days) · sprint completion rate · weekly effort trend.

## Common traps
- Waiting to feel motivated before starting.
- Using motivational videos as a substitute for starting.
- Rewarding yourself only for outcomes you don't control.
