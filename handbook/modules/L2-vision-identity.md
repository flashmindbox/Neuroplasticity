# L2 · Vision & identity

[← Handbook](../README.md)

**Dimensions served:** 4 Vision & purpose, 13 Integrity & civil-servant identity, 5 Motivation.
**Evidence grade:** B (process simulation, mental contrasting/WOOP) · A (implementation intentions) · B (identity & values-based self-worth).

## Why it matters for UPSC
"Why do you want to join the civil services?" is the most predictable interview question — and the most
revealing. A concrete, owned vision sustains effort and makes Essay and GS4 answers authentic. But the wrong
kind of visualisation backfires.

## How it works
- **Positive fantasies alone reduce effort.** Dreaming about the outcome can feel like having achieved it
  (Oettingen, 2012).
- **Mental contrasting** — imagining the desired future *and then* the obstacle in reality — energises action;
  combined with implementation intentions it is called **WOOP** (Wish, Outcome, Obstacle, Plan) and improved
  students' preparation (Duckworth et al., 2013).
- **Process simulation beats outcome simulation.** Students who rehearsed *how* they would study did better
  than those who imagined the grade (Pham & Taylor, 1999).
- **Identity drives behaviour**, but identity tied to one outcome is fragile (Crocker & Park, 2004). Anchor
  identity in values and behaviours you control.

## Exercises & dose

### 1. Purpose statement (B) — written in Phase 0; revised monthly
Answer in under 150 words: Who do I want to serve? What problem do I want to work on? What kind of officer do I
want people to meet? Why me? Read it each morning (30 seconds).

### 2. The district picture (B) — once per phase, 20 min
Write a concrete day as a District Magistrate or in your target service: a specific problem (a flood, a land
dispute, a failing scheme), the people involved, your decisions, the trade-offs. Concrete beats grand.

### 3. Process simulation (B) — 2 min every morning
Rehearse *today's hardest task*: where you'll sit, how you'll start, the moment you'll want to quit, what you'll
do then (your if-then), how you'll feel finishing it.

### 4. WOOP (B) — start of every 2-week sprint; any important goal
- **Wish:** challenging but feasible, for this sprint.
- **Outcome:** the best result — feel it briefly.
- **Obstacle:** the main *inner* obstacle (habit, emotion, belief) — be honest.
- **Plan:** "If [obstacle], then I will [action]."

### 5. Identity evidence log (B) — whenever it happens
"I am becoming someone who…" + the evidence ("kept my 6:00 wake time on a bad day", "admitted a mistake in my
study circle"). Votes for the person you are becoming, tagged by dimension.

### 6. Values-based identity & Plan B (B) — Phase 0, revisit quarterly
Write 3–5 values you want to live by regardless of results. Write your Plan B. Your identity is the values;
the exam is one way to express them.

## Progression
- **L1** Purpose statement written.
- **L2** Daily process simulation; WOOP each sprint.
- **L3** District picture is vivid and connects to daily tasks; evidence log active.
- **L4** You can articulate your "why" convincingly in 60 seconds; identity is stable through setbacks.

## Objective markers
Purpose statement revisions · WOOPs completed · evidence-log entries/week · 60-second "why" talk (AI/external rated).

## Common traps
- Vision boards of the outcome (LBSNAA, the rank list) instead of the process.
- A purpose copied from toppers' interviews — it must be yours.
- Making "IAS officer" your whole identity.
