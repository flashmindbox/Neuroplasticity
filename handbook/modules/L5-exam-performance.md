# L5 · Exam performance

[← Handbook](../README.md)

**Dimensions served:** 1 Deep focus, 3 Stress, 9 Judgement, 10 Expression, 11 Communication.
**Evidence grade:** A (stress inoculation; specificity; sleep) · B (arousal reappraisal; implementation intentions for contingencies) · Analogy (tapering) · C (expressive writing).

## Why it matters for UPSC
Preparation is converted into marks only on a few specific days. This module trains the performer: composure,
pacing, decisions under uncertainty, stamina, and recovery between papers.

## How it works
- **Rehearse under realistic conditions** (stress inoculation — Saunders et al., 1996): same times, same
  duration, same materials, same pressure.
- **Plan responses to disruption in advance** (implementation intentions — Gollwitzer & Sheeran, 2006).
- **Arrive rested**: sleep in the days before the exam matters more than last-minute content.
- **Tapering** (analogy from sport — Bosquet et al., 2007): reduce load in the final ~10 days to arrive fresh.

## Prelims mind
- **Mock routine at exam time** (9:30 GS, 14:30 CSAT), including travel time, breakfast and the gap between papers.
- **Pass strategy**: first pass — attempt Sure and Likely; second pass — 50-50s per your personal attempt rule;
  final minutes — fill OMR carefully. Practise the strategy you'll actually use.
- **Confidence tagging** on every mock ([Judgement & calibration](L3-judgement-calibration.md)).
- **One-question rule**: if a question takes more than ~90 seconds on the first pass, mark it and move on.
- **CSAT**: treat as a real paper; practise under time.
- **Pre-mock routine**: 3 min cyclic sighing → arousal reappraisal script → (optional, grade C) 10-min worry writing.
- **Post-mock**: 15-minute review using the failure protocol — not a verdict on your worth.

## Mains mind
- **3-hour simulations**, building to **two per day**, and once to a **5-day rehearsal** in July.
- **Pacing**: decide time per question in advance; practise moving on.
- **Between-paper recovery** (lunch break): eat, walk, breathe, *no post-mortem* of the morning paper.
- **Evening recovery** during Mains week: light review only, sleep at your fixed time.
- **Stamina marker**: quality of your last 30 minutes vs the first 30 (self-rated + structure rubric).

## Exam-day if-then plans (write by 1 May; review weekly)
- If I **blank** on a question → I breathe out slowly twice, mark it, move on, return later.
- If I **panic** → I name it ("this is anxiety"), do three cyclic sighs, and read the next question.
- If I'm **behind time** → I switch to skeleton answers for the remaining questions (Mains) / attempt only Sure and Likely (Prelims).
- If the **paper format changes** → I remind myself it changed for everyone and apply the same principles.
- If I **can't sleep** the night before → I rest with eyes closed; one poor night barely affects performance.
- If **something goes wrong in travel** → backup route and documents prepared the day before.

## Waiting periods
Between Prelims and results, Mains and results, interview and final list: keep structure (wake time, exercise,
programme), limit result-forum time to a fixed window, and keep moving forward (start Mains/interview work)
regardless.

## Personality Test
See [Communication & presence](L4-communication.md) and [Leadership & integrity](L4-leadership-integrity.md).
Final weeks: AI mock boards, a few human mock boards, sleep, calm.

## Progression
- **L1** Mocks taken at random times; no routine.
- **L2** Mock routine at exam times; pre-mock routine used.
- **L3** If-then plans written and rehearsed; 3-hour simulations with stable quality.
- **L4** 5-day rehearsal done; performance on mock days matches practice.

## Objective markers
Mock-day vs practice performance · last-30-minute quality drop · calibration on mocks · sleep in exam weeks.

## Common traps
- Cramming new content in the taper.
- Post-mortems between papers.
- Changing strategy on the day.
